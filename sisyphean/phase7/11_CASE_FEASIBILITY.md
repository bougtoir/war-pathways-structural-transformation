## Germany 1945–49 (US/UK/French zones)

- Sources: occupation fiscal reports (OMGUS), reparations accounts,
  dismantling records, forced trade balances — richest documentary
  base of any case.
- What exists: A channels largely documented; C documented; V_pre/post
  available (Maddison/economic histories).
- Problem: multiple occupiers → attribution of flows per zone needed;
  reparations-in-kind vs production double-counting risk.
- Verdict: feasible for original coding — **the cleanest case**.

## Japan 1945–52

- Sources: SCAP records, reparations surveys, fiscal accounts.
- What exists: strong documentation; BUT extraction was deliberately
  limited (reconstruction policy) — an occupation designed not to
  appropriate is a boundary case for sign(π∞), interesting precisely
  because π ≈ −C < 0.
- Problem: the case tests policy choice more than structural
  appropriability.
- Verdict: feasible — informative as the *negative* arm.

## Kuwait 1990–91

- Sources: UN Compensation Commission records (documented
  claims/reparations), Iraqi asset-seizure reports.
- What exists: seizure inventories partially documented; short episode
  (7 months) → censored case; wartime destruction contaminates V_post.
- Verdict: feasible at sign level only — physical-resource-dominant
  case, key positive arm for the mix test.
