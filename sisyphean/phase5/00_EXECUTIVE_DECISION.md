# 00 — Executive Decision (Phase 5)

**CONDITIONAL GO — one narrow, testable contribution; NOT a new general
theory.**

Phase 5 reoriented the program from "make war an inferior instrument"
(A, subordinate) to "make coercive appropriation structurally
ineffective" (B, superordinate). Findings:

- The reframe is legitimate but the mechanism space is heavily occupied:
  hold-up/incomplete contracts (Klein-Crawford-Alchian, Hart-Moore),
  predation theory (Grossman-Kim, Hirshleifer), conquest economics
  (Liberman), commercial peace (Gartzke-Li-Boehmer), sanction theory
  (Baldwin). The surviving contribution is an **asset-mix-conditioned
  appropriability model**: whether coercion pays is governed by the
  share of produced value that is consent-dependent, not by coercion
  intensity itself.
- In simulation this yields one non-trivial emergent result: coercion
  of consent-dependent assets ends in "unprofitable" exits (p=1.0 for
  human/institutional/network mixes) while coercion of physical assets
  does not (p=0) — with onsets still occurring for all mixes
  (appropriability loss does NOT prevent aggression).
- Hard negatives preserved: intrinsically motivated and
  regime-survival actors are NOT deterred (longest spells under those
  rules); war-economy/sponsor-rich settings remain permanent failures;
  displacement can again erase benefits.
- Empirical: appropriability/consent are not directly measured by any
  public dataset; the pilot gate stopped at a feasibility audit — one
  defensible design (SIPRI/UCDP sponsor-externality test from Phase 4
  Gate 39) remains.

**Strongest insight:** the "does conquest pay" question should be
re-asked conditionally on the asset composition of the target — Liberman
answered it for industrial-physical assets; the consent-dependent share
is where coercion fails.

**Best counterexample:** Liberman's core finding (industrial conquest
did pay) — physical assets are capturable, and our model reproduces
that arm.

**Next test:** the Phase-4 sponsor-externality duration design; strict
stopping rule — if no distinguishing sponsor-side effect on duration
appears, the synthesis terminates.
