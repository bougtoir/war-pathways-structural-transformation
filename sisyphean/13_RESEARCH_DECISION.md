# 13 — Research Decision

## Decision: CONDITIONAL GO

## (a) Strongest defensible claim

Aggressive war can be made structurally self-limiting *along the
sustainment margin* when (i) a causally necessary stock K exists, (ii) war
onset raises K-burn above replenishment, and (iii) replenishment is
bounded. Under those conditions onset endogenously shortens war (~40% in
the reference calibration) and roughly halves civilian harm — without
degrading defender capacity, civilian functionality, or requiring any
maximizing decision rule. This is the lever: hard to start, self-eroding
once started.

## (b) Exact assumptions the claim requires

- K-replenishment (sponsors + stockpile + substitution) is **bounded
  below burn**: ρ < κ·c·m + r_b·d. The claim is void outside this region.
- Aggressor/defender asymmetry is enforceable: mechanisms can target the
  aggressor's K without touching defender sustainment (assumed, not
  derived).
- Onset barriers are per-attempt gates; they delay and filter stochastic
  attempts but do not prevent deliberate onsets over long horizons.
- Civilian protection level protect ≥ ~0.6 kept the constraint slack;
  results degrade if enforcement weakens.

## (c) Closest prior claim and the difference

Powell (2012) "Persistent Fighting and Shifting Power" and Slantchev
(2004): war self-terminates as power shifts / costs accumulate. Difference:
the lever names a *measurable stock* whose burn is onset-triggered, gives
a verifiable finite-lag bound, and is evaluated across non-maximizing
selection rules while holding civilians out of the mechanism. Novelty =
synthesis + the explicit bounded-replenishment falsifying region (02).

## (d) Most damaging counterexample

A sponsor-rich aggressor (external resourcing scaling with aggression):
replenishment ≥ burn → no K-exit ever (observed p_K_exit = 0, spells to
cap, civilian_ok = 0). Proxy war fought on someone else's budget is the
real-world instance; the lever addresses it only via cutting ρ, which is
the sanctions problem it does not solve.

## (e) Best falsifiable prediction

Cross-conflict: war duration correlates with the ratio of aggressor-side
external supply inflow to per-period materiel burn — *not* with grievance
intensity — and wars show a characteristic duration ceiling ≈
(K₀ + stockpile)/(burn − inflow) where inflow is bounded. If sponsors
rescale inflow, the ceiling disappears.

## (f) What must remain unclaimed

- No universal war-prevention or near-zero onset claims (P(hit W) stays
  ~1 over long horizons under tested barriers).
- No claim of rationality-independence at the *onset* level (onset
  frequency remains rule-dependent); only sustainment is rule-agnostic.
- No claim on displaced/sub-state violence reduction — it is a cost,
  possibly rising when onset is blocked.
- No claim on defensive-war impairment: unmodeled preemption/first-strike
  incentives remain open.
- No claim that K can be identified ex ante in real states — the
  operational measurability of the stock is future empirical work.
