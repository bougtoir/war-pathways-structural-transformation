# 04 — Asset Composition Dynamics (Gate 3)

Two timescales kept separate: **fast** conflict decisions (r chosen
per episode; L response within episodes) and **slow** asset-share drift
(w_phys, w_hum, w_inst, w_net re-weighted by investment, relocation,
substitution, network reconfiguration).

## Slow-share dynamics (conceptual, not imposed)

- Investment: societies accumulating physical capital raise w_phys;
  human/relational investment raises consent weights. Direction is a
  choice under prices and security — **no secular drift toward low
  appropriability is assumed**.
- Relocation: mobile human capital emigrating under coercion risk
  lowers w_hum domestically — can RAISE the residual physical share,
  making the rump *more* appropriable. Counterintuitive, kept.
- Substitution: physical assets replaced by contract-/network-based
  production lowers w_phys (commercial development channel).
- Network reconfiguration: networks can re-form around coercers or
  exclude them — direction not stipulated.

## Joint movement of creation and extraction

V and α need not move independently: coercion that suppresses V can
simultaneously compress α (networks collapse) or expand it (forced
industrial output adds physical value). The model treats (w, V) as
co-evolving; no assumed orthogonality.

## Two supported stable regimes (c6_regimes.csv)

| regime | composition | spec | π∞(r=1) | reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| high-appropriability | w_phys = 1 | rule | +0.040 | coercion pays — stable capturable regime |
| low-appropriability | consent-heavy (w_phys = .05) | opt/rule | −0.32 to −0.36 | coercion unprofitable — stable |

The high-appropriability regime is stable in the precise sense that no
endogenous force in the model moves w_phys down; symmetrically for the
consent regime. Which regime a society occupies is a slow-history
outcome, not a theorem's conclusion.
