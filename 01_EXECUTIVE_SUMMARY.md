# 01 — Executive Summary

**Project:** Structural Transformation of War Pathways — can the causal
pathways from political disagreement to organized mass violence be
structurally transformed so that large-scale violence becomes
unnecessary, infeasible, or unsustainable — without assuming rational or
benevolent actors?

**Verdict: CONDITIONAL GO.** A defensible *integrative* contribution
exists; no component is novel; claims must be scoped (see 14).

## The framework in one paragraph

War is modeled as a **conjunctive pathway** through six analytical
stages — conflict formation → bargaining failure → selection of force →
mobilization feasibility → escalation → system adaptation — where the
decision to fight rests with a *leader* whose utility diverges from the
state's, the regime's, and the population's. Because selection,
feasibility, and escalation are separate probabilistic gates,
interventions at different stages multiply rather than add. Structural
transformation means durably narrowing any gate; the framework predicts
which mechanism binds at which gate.

## Key constructions

- **War-rationality regions** R_state, R_regime, R_leader, R_population
  over the (stake × victory-probability) square. Baseline (illustrative):
  |R_leader| = 9.3% ≫ |R_state| = 0.3%; a ~9-point "agency gap" — wars
  the leader wants that the state's material calculus rejects.
- **Mobilization gate** M(θ) ≥ M_req(G): opportunity cost of civilian
  production is the lever; it barely moves leader preference but removes
  ~25% of feasibility in the illustrative run.
- **Adaptive stability:** under replicator-dynamics metaphor, nonviolent
  substitution is an ESS only if the substitution premium beats the
  exploitation wedge; at exploit=0.70 no pure peace is stable — a
  permanent ~18% latent aggression remains.
- **Network escalation:** conflict cascades are edge-type-dependent —
  stabilizing ties dominate topology (s̄=0.2 alone cuts cascades ~97%);
  connectivity is non-monotone.

## Findings that discipline the thesis

1. Interventions are stage-specific: alternatives quality binds at
   bargaining, alignment at selection, opportunity cost at mobilization,
   amplification at escalation. Combined interventions eliminate the
   pathway only because every gate narrows at once.
2. The highest-leverage single intervention on *selection* is
   state–regime incentive alignment (δ): it collapses the leader-only
   region most steeply.
3. Nonviolent structures are *conditionally* stable — they require a
   retained defense floor and shock magnitudes below the basin edge.
   "War obsolescence" survives only as shrinkable regions plus
   conditional resilience, never as permanent elimination.
4. Hostile review concessions (mandatory): claims are
   state-rationality-independent, not rationality-independent;
   obsolescence is scoped to interstate mass violence with relocation
   tracking; region size is construct-level, bound-identifiable only.
